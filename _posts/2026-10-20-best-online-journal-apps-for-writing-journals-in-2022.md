Writing a journal is a way of reflecting on your problems and your limits. It is also an exercise in self-knowledge. When you write a journal there is nobody to impress and nothing to get right — just a blank page and no judgement.

An online journal app makes the habit much easier to keep. It is with you wherever your phone is, it searches years of entries in a second, and it cannot be lost or read by whoever finds it in a drawer. Below are five we think are worth your time this year, and what each one is genuinely good at.

<h2 id="goodnight-journal">Goodnight Journal</h2>

![Goodnight Journal](/images/post/goodnightjournal.png)

<p><b>Goodnight Journal is simple and intuitive, which is most of what makes a journal app worth opening.</b> The writing surface is quiet, your entries save as you go, and everything is private unless you decide otherwise.</p>
<p>What really separates it from the other apps here is the community. You can keep every entry to yourself, or publish one and find that other people have felt exactly what you are feeling. Writing something difficult and discovering you are not the only one is a different experience from writing into a locked box, and for a lot of people it is the thing that keeps the habit alive.</p>
<p>The interface is also consistent across desktop, phone and tablet, so the journal works the same way wherever you happen to be when you want to write. <b>If you want simplicity and the option of a community, this is the one to try first.</b></p>
<a href="https://www.goodnightjournal.com/" target="_blank" class="btn-cta">Goodnight Journal</a>

<hr />

<h2 id="day-one">Day One</h2>

![Day One](/images/post/dayone.png)

<p>Day One is the polished, media-heavy option, refined over many years. Entries hold text, photos, video, voice memos and location, and the app is good at resurfacing what you wrote on this day in previous years — which turns out to be one of journaling's real pleasures.</p>
<p>It is strongest on Apple devices, though Android and web versions exist. If your instinct is closer to scrapbooking than to diary-keeping, start here.</p>
<a href="https://dayoneapp.com/" target="_blank" class="btn-cta">Day One</a>

<hr />

<h2 id="daylio">Daylio</h2>

<p>Daylio is the app for people who like the idea of journaling but never find the time. Rather than a blank page, it asks you to pick your mood and tag what you were doing. That is a finished entry, and it takes about ten seconds. You can add a written note when you feel like it.</p>
<p>Keep it up for a few months and the charts become the point: you can see plainly which activities lift your mood and which ones flatten it. It is the easiest habit on this list to sustain, because it survives the days when you have nothing to say.</p>
<a href="https://daylio.net/" target="_blank" class="btn-cta">Daylio</a>

<hr />

<h2 id="penzu">Penzu</h2>

![Penzu](/images/post/penzu.png)

<p>Penzu is stress-free journaling in the most literal sense. The writing view is deliberately plain, the emphasis is on keeping entries locked down, and there are no social features to think about. It has been around long enough to be genuinely dependable.</p>
<p>If what you want is somewhere private to write and nothing else, Penzu does that and gets out of the way.</p>
<a href="https://penzu.com/" target="_blank" class="btn-cta">Penzu</a>

<hr />

<h2 id="diaro">Diaro</h2>

<p>Diaro is for people who want their journal organised. Every entry can live in a folder and carry tags, so a year of writing stays navigable rather than becoming one long pile. There is a distraction-free writing mode, and cloud sync so your entries follow you to a new device.</p>
<p>If you want to pull up everything you have written about one subject across several years, Diaro makes that straightforward.</p>
<a href="https://diaroapp.com/" target="_blank" class="btn-cta">Diaro</a>

<hr />

<h3>So which one should you pick?</h3>
<p>The best online journal is the one you will still be opening in three months, and that comes down to whatever currently gets in your way. If it is time, use Daylio. If you want your photos and places woven in, use Day One. If you want structure, use Diaro. If you want a sealed private diary, use Penzu. If you want privacy with the option of being heard, use <a href="https://www.goodnightjournal.com/"><b>Goodnight Journal</b></a>.</p>
<p>All of them let you start writing without committing to anything, so try two or three for a week each. The one you open without thinking about it is your answer.</p>
