<p class="disclosure"><strong>Disclosure:</strong> we run Goodnight Journal, one of the apps recommended below. We have tried to be fair and specific about what every app here does well, but you should weigh our recommendation knowing that.</p>

<p>Almost every journaling app is a sealed box. You write, it saves, nobody ever sees it. That is the right design for most people, and it is what the vast majority of reviews assume you want.</p>

<p>But a real and under-served group of writers want something else: somewhere private to write that is <em>not</em> solitary. Somewhere you can post the occasional entry and find that other people have felt the same thing. If you grew up on LiveJournal, Xanga or Open Diary, you already know the appeal, and you have probably noticed how few places still offer it.</p>

<p>This is a short list because the category is genuinely small. Here is what is actually still running.</p>

<h2>Important: Open Diary has closed</h2>

<p>If you came here looking for Open Diary, it is gone. The site was scheduled to shut down at the end of February 2026, with the deadline extended to give members time to download their diaries.</p>

<p>If you still have entries there and never exported them, the ProseBox team built a downloader for Open Diary journals — that is the first place to look. Archive Team's wiki page on Open Diary is the other.</p>

<p>This matters beyond nostalgia: Open Diary ran for roughly twenty-five years and still closed. If you are choosing somewhere to keep a decade of your life, ask whether you can get your entries <em>out</em> before you put them in.</p>

<h2>Goodnight Journal</h2>

![Goodnight Journal](/images/post/goodnightjournal.png)

<p>Entries are private by default, and the sharing decision is made one entry at a time rather than as an account-wide setting. That distinction matters more than it sounds: you can keep years of writing completely private and still publish the one entry you want other people to see.</p>

<p>The writing surface is quiet, there is no advertising next to your diary, and the same interface works across desktop, phone and tablet, so an entry started on a laptop can be finished on a phone. There is an iOS app as well as the web version.</p>

<p>In practice the appeal is narrow and strong. You write something you assumed was uniquely strange or shameful, you post it, and several people turn out to have written almost exactly the same thing. That is the thing that keeps people journaling on the days they would otherwise skip, and it is not available from a locked notebook. A paid membership exists and adds more community features; the private writing is not what sits behind it.</p>

<a href="https://www.goodnightjournal.com/" target="_blank" class="btn-cta">Check out Goodnight Journal</a>

<h2>ProseBox</h2>

<p>ProseBox is the natural home for displaced Open Diary users, and it has earned that position — it built the import tool that lets people bring their old entries across. It is free to use on the web.</p>

<p>The community is the point here, and it is active. The writing is long-form and unvarnished in a way that modern social platforms have mostly lost. If what you miss is the early-2000s diary internet rather than a polished app, this is the closest thing to it that still exists.</p>

<p>The trade is that it is web-only and the design is plain. Nobody is choosing ProseBox for its interface.</p>

<a href="https://www.prosebox.net/" target="_blank" class="btn-cta">Check out ProseBox</a>

<h2>My Diary</h2>

![My Diary](/images/post/mydiary.png)

<p>One of the older online journals, still running and still free. You can write both publicly and privately, which is rare enough to earn it a place on this list.</p>

<p>The honest caveat is that the community side is thin. You can publish, but there is not much in the way of responding to each other, so it functions more as public writing than as a community. The design shows its age too. If you want somewhere simple and free to write in the open, it works; if you want the back-and-forth, look at the two above.</p>

<a href="https://www.my-diary.org/" target="_blank" class="btn-cta">Check out My Diary</a>

<h2>Choosing one</h2>

<p>Three questions settle it.</p>

<p><b>Do you want privacy first, with sharing as an option?</b> Then you want per-entry control, and <a href="https://www.goodnightjournal.com/"><b>Goodnight Journal</b></a> is built around exactly that.</p>

<p><b>Do you want the old diary internet back?</b> ProseBox, without much competition.</p>

<p><b>Do you want to write in the open, simply and for free?</b> My Diary does that.</p>

<p>And whichever you pick, find the export button before you need it. Open Diary's closure is a reminder that a journal you cannot get out of a service is a journal you have lent rather than kept.</p>
